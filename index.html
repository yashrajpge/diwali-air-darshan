Crackers: burn and fire safety

This is safety information only. It does not say crackers are allowed or safe, and gives no safe number to burst. Check local rules and orders for your city first.
Protect eyesWear protective eyeglasses when lighting or closely watching fireworks.
Source: MoHFW DGHS 2026 guidelines.
Keep water or sand readyKeep a bucket of water or sand nearby. Put used fireworks into water or dry sand.
Sources: MoHFW DGHS 2026; Maharashtra Fire and Emergency Services.
Closed footwearWear closed footwear, especially around ground chakris.
Source: MoHFW DGHS 2026.
ClothingTie loose dupatta or chunni snugly. Avoid loose, flowing or synthetic clothes. Wear tight cotton clothes.
Sources: MoHFW DGHS 2026; Maharashtra Fire Services.
Children only with adultsOnly adults light fireworks, or children under close adult supervision.
Sources: MoHFW DGHS 2026; Maharashtra Fire Services.
One at a time, from a distanceLight one firework at a time. Use a long candle or phooljhari to light, and keep your arm straight.
Source: MoHFW DGHS 2026.
Never hold or relightDo not hold an anar or bomb in the hand. Do not relight one that failed. Do not put crackers in a container.
Sources: MoHFW DGHS 2026; Maharashtra Fire Services.
If someone is burntMove them away from the source, put out any flames on clothing, then cool the burn with clean running water and get medical help. Eye injuries need medical care.
Sources: MoHFW DGHS 2026 (first aid chapter); Maharashtra Fire Services.
Fire emergencyIn case of fire, dial 101 (fire service). For a serious injury, get professional medical help at once.
Source: NIDM, Dos and Don'ts for Hazards (fire).

Bursting and air quality

  • Check your city's and state's rules on firecrackers first. Many places restrict them or ban them.
  • When the AQI category is poor, children, older people and people with breathing problems should stay indoors.
  • This site does not give a "safe number" of crackers. No data source supports one.

About this site

  • Cities: Pune and Latur first, then other Maharashtra cities and the big metros.
  • Station names come from the CPCB list of continuous monitoring stations. Listed does not mean reporting today.
  • Air values shown are modelled PM2.5 and PM10 estimates from Open-Meteo (CAMS global model, about 45 km grid, CC BY 4.0, non-commercial use). They are not CPCB station readings and not the Indian AQI, and can differ a lot from what a station measures.
  • Festival dates for 2026 per calendar sources: Dhanteras 6 Nov, Lakshmi Puja 8 Nov, Bhai Dooj 11 Nov.
